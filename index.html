Platform Investigation

Linkby

What It Is

Linkby is a performance-based adtech platform founded in Sydney in 2020. It functions as a digital PR tool, connecting brands with 600+ premium publishers for editorial-style content. Brands pay only when readers click through, with no retainers and no upfront costs.[1]

The founders, Chris Wirasinha, Adrian Fagerlund, and Andrew Chak, previously co-founded Pedestrian.tv, Australia's largest youth publishing platform. This gave them direct experience on both the brand and publisher sides of the relationship.[2]

Linkby as a Digital PR Tool

At its core, Linkby is a digital PR tool. That is the most accurate way to describe what it does and how it should be evaluated.

Traditional PR means pitching journalists, building relationships, and hoping for coverage. There are no guarantees, and results can take months. Linkby replaces that uncertainty with a structured, pay-for-performance model. A brand submits a brief, publishers opt in to write editorial-style content, and the brand pays only when someone clicks through.[11]

How It Differs from Traditional PR
With traditional PR, you pay a retainer regardless of results. With Linkby, you pay only when the content drives real traffic. The coverage still appears in legitimate publications with real editorial standards. The difference is accountability.

It is worth noting that some PR professionals find this model uncomfortable. Publishers already weigh traffic and commercial value when deciding what to cover. Linkby simply makes that dynamic transparent and puts a price on it.[11]

For brands without an established PR agency relationship, or those launching a specific product and needing fast, targeted coverage, Linkby fills a real gap. It is not a replacement for a full PR strategy, but it is a practical, lower-risk way to get into publications that would otherwise require months of relationship building.

Relevance to AI Generative Search

As LLMs like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini increasingly surface answers drawn from trusted editorial sources, brands mentioned inside premium publisher articles gain passive discoverability in AI-generated results. Linkby places brand content within outlets like Morning Brew, Vogue, Wired, and The Skimm. These are exactly the types of sources these models are trained to draw from.

Why This Matters
Traditional SEO is increasingly disrupted by AI-generated summaries that bypass brand websites entirely. Editorial placements in authoritative publications are becoming one of the most reliable signals for AI discoverability. Linkby offers a performance-based, cost-controlled way to secure them.

Linkby publicly positions itself around the emerging GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) trend, where brands are diversifying spend away from pure SEO and toward trusted editorial channels that feed AI models.[1] For a brand wanting to appear in AI-generated product recommendations or "best of" roundups, this is an increasingly strategic lever.

Impact Platform Compatibility

Some AI-generated answers describe Linkby as compatible with affiliate-style platforms like Impact. This framing is understandable given how Linkby functions, but it requires an important clarification before acting on it.

Key Clarification
There is no publicly documented native Impact integration for Linkby. According to industry analysis, Linkby does not live on traditional affiliate platforms like AWIN or Impact. It operates its own closed marketplace, and its payout model is based on clicks rather than conversions, which is a fundamentally different structure from standard affiliate tracking.[11]

This creates a real challenge for affiliate managers. Because the model pays per click rather than per sale, it is harder to measure ROAS in the traditional sense and cannot be reported through an existing Impact dashboard without custom workarounds.[11]

Linkby sits in its own category. It is not quite PR, not quite affiliate. That is part of what makes it interesting, and part of what makes it difficult to fit into an existing team structure or reporting workflow.[11]

Business Health and Traction

Linkby raised $15M in Series B funding in July 2025, led by Boston-based Volition Capital, bringing total funding to approximately $26M.[1][3] The U.S. is now the platform's largest market at 55% of revenue, with some months showing 100% year-over-year growth.[4]

Over 3,800 consumer brands are active on the platform, including Charlotte Tilbury, Beyond Meat, Skechers, Free People, T-Mobile, and Lululemon.[1]

"Linkby is building a performance-driven solution for brands to scale through trusted editorial. By bringing accountability and efficiency to a traditionally high-cost, opaque part of the media mix, Linkby has unlocked a new channel for customer acquisition." Jim Ferry, Partner, Volition Capital [1]

Budgets can be split across multiple publishers. For example, $10,000 across two titles at $5,000 each. Setting a higher CPC incentivises more publishers to accept a campaign brief. A CPM product called Pubfeed is also available for infinite-scroll placements across publisher sites.[5]

The publisher minimum budget is the key figure to watch. Even at $1 per click, onboarding a publisher means committing up to $10,000 if they fully deliver. Multi-publisher campaigns can scale up quickly.

Publisher Network

Linkby performs best when content is distributed through owned and operated publisher channels such as newsletters and homepage placements. Strong performers in the network include Morning Brew, The Skimm, The Daily Aus, and creator-led Substacks.[4] These are established, high-quality outlets.

A newer "reverse pitching" feature lets publishers pitch directly to brands, rather than only the other way around.[6] Publisher opt-in is never guaranteed, and brands in niche or less commercially attractive categories may receive limited interest.

Key Risks

Risk Detail
Click Quality The click farm allegation is the most serious concern.[7] Linkby's fraud protection is proprietary and has not been independently verified. Always validate with UTM tracking, ideally through Impact, before scaling spend.
Publisher Opt-In Publishers decide whether to accept a brief.[9] Niche or low-commercial-appeal brands may see limited or no takers, which effectively blocks campaign delivery.
Upper Funnel Only Linkby drives awareness and editorial traffic. It is not a direct-response or conversion channel and should not be used as a substitute for performance paid media.[7]
Analytics Discrepancy Some users report clicks recorded in the Linkby dashboard that do not appear in GA4 or other analytics tools. This creates attribution disputes that can be difficult to resolve without independent tracking in place.
Impact Integration No native Impact integration is publicly documented. Confirm with Linkby directly before assuming seamless compatibility with an existing Impact workflow.
Budget Commitment Publisher minimum budgets of $2,500 to $10,000 per title mean multi-publisher campaigns can become expensive quickly, despite the pay-per-click framing.

The Opportunity

Linkby occupies a genuine and growing gap in the market. As AI-generated search summaries increasingly bypass brand websites, editorial placements in trusted publications are becoming more strategically valuable.[4] Linkby is one of the few platforms offering a performance-based path into these channels at scale.

The platform is well suited to product launches, seasonal campaigns, and building the kind of authoritative editorial presence that AI models draw from when generating recommendations.[1] For brands that want coverage in Vogue, Wired, or Marie Claire without committing to a PR retainer, it is a compelling alternative worth testing.

Bottom Line

Linkby is a legitimate, well-funded platform with genuine traction and a use case that is becoming more relevant as AI generative search reshapes how consumers discover brands. As a digital PR tool, it offers a cost-controlled alternative to traditional PR retainers with the added benefit of building an editorial footprint that feeds LLMs.

The polarised reviews, particularly around click quality, mean a small pilot is essential before committing to scale. Start with one publisher, set a capped budget, and run UTM tracking through Impact or independently before launch.

If click numbers reconcile with your own analytics, it is worth expanding. Confirm Impact compatibility directly with the Linkby team before signing anything.
