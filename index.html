Platform Investigation

Linkby

Prepared for Liam  ·  May 2026  ·  Performance Adtech

What It Is

Linkby is an Australian-founded (2020) performance-based adtech platform that sits between traditional PR and digital advertising. It lets brands pitch directly to over 600 premium publishers and pay only when readers engage, using cost-per-click (CPC) or CPM pricing.[1] The core pitch: get editorial-style media coverage without upfront costs or retainers.

The founders — Chris Wirasinha, Adrian Fagerlund, and Andrew Chak — built Linkby after co-founding Pedestrian.tv, Australia's biggest youth publishing platform, giving them direct insight into both sides of the brand-publisher relationship.[2]

Business Health & Traction

The company is well-funded and growing fast. Linkby raised $15M in Series B funding in July 2025, led by Boston-based Volition Capital, to support U.S. expansion and continued product development.[1] Total funding across all rounds now sits at approximately $26M.[3]

The U.S. is now Linkby's largest market, accounting for 55% of revenue, with some months seeing 100% growth year-over-year.[4] The brand roster is credible: more than 3,800 consumer brands use the platform, including Charlotte Tilbury, Beyond Meat, Skechers, Free People, T-Mobile, and Lululemon.[1]

"Linkby is building a performance-driven solution for brands to scale through trusted editorial. By bringing accountability and efficiency to a traditionally high-cost, opaque part of the media mix, Linkby has unlocked a new channel for customer acquisition." — Jim Ferry, Partner, Volition Capital[1]

You can split larger budgets across multiple publishers (e.g. $10,000 across two titles at $5,000 each). Setting a higher CPC incentivises more publishers to accept your campaign brief. There's also a CPM product called Pubfeed for infinite-scroll placements across publisher sites.[5]

The minimum allocated budget per publisher is the key number to watch — even at $1/click, getting a publisher on board effectively means committing up to $2,500–$10,000 if they fully deliver. That's not nothing.

Publisher Network

Linkby performs best when content is shared across owned and operated publisher channels like newsletters and homepage placements. Standout performers in the network include Morning Brew, The Skimm, The Daily Aus, and creator-led Substacks.[4] These are legitimate, high-quality outlets — not content farms.

A new "reverse pitching" feature now allows interested publishers to pitch directly to brands, rather than solely the other way around.[6] Publisher opt-in is not guaranteed — brands in niche or less commercially attractive categories may receive fewer takers.

Sentiment & Reviews

Trustpilot: 4.1/5 (27 reviews). The majority of reviews are five-star, dominated by long-term users citing 3–4 years of partnership. Multiple brands report strong ROI — one cited campaigns achieving up to 3x ROAS,[7] and another reported a 56% sales uplift with a key retail partner.[8] The breakdown is notably polarised: 70% five-star, 19% one-star, with almost nothing in between — a flag worth noting.

The main negative complaint: One reviewer alleged that Linkby publishers use click farms, generating clicks that don't appear in the brand's own traffic analytics, and reported that customer service was unresponsive — only engaging when trying to sell another campaign.[7] Linkby replied publicly and said they were investigating once the user provided data.

G2: One reviewer noted "no publications picked up our story, despite following the Linkby guidelines,"[9] confirming that publisher participation is not guaranteed — a real risk if your category is niche.

Glassdoor (employee reviews): 3.6/5 — 51% would recommend to a friend; senior management scores only 2.6/5.[10] One review warned of high stress and burnout in sales roles, while others cited good culture and flexibility.

Key Risks to Flag

Risk Detail
Click quality The click farm allegation[7] is the most serious concern. Their fraud protection is proprietary and unverified externally. Recommend independent UTM tracking before scaling.
Publisher opt-in not guaranteed Publishers choose whether to accept your brief.[9] Niche or low-commercial-appeal brands may get limited takers.
Upper-funnel by nature One agency reviewer noted explicitly: "the activity is still very upper funnel."[7] Not a direct-response channel.
Analytics discrepancy Some users report clicks not appearing in their own analytics, creating attribution confusion and disputes.
Minimum budgets add up At $2,500–$10,000 per publisher minimum, multi-publisher campaigns can get expensive despite the "pay per click" framing.

The Opportunity

Linkby occupies a genuine gap. As SEO-based strategies that relied on traditional affiliate models struggle, owned and operated publisher channels are growing.[4] The platform is also positioning itself around the emerging GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) trend — as discoverability by LLMs like ChatGPT increasingly favours premium publishers, brands are looking to diversify spend toward performance-based editorial models.[1]

For a brand wanting editorial presence in publications like Vogue, Marie Claire, or Wired without a PR retainer, the concept has real merit — particularly for product launches and seasonal campaigns (Black Friday, Mother's Day, etc.).[7]

Bottom Line for Liam

Linkby is a legitimate, well-funded platform with genuine traction and recognisable brand clients. It's best suited for D2C/e-commerce brands focused on awareness and traffic, not direct conversion. The performance model is genuinely lower-risk than traditional PR retainers,[7] but the polarised reviews — particularly the click quality complaints — warrant requesting a pilot with a small publisher and tight budget first before scaling spend.

Set up UTM tracking carefully before launch to independently verify click quality against what Linkby's dashboard reports. If numbers reconcile, scale with confidence.
